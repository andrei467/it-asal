SECTION A — Multiple choice [14 marks]

Choose one answer for each question.

Question 11 mark

Which statement best describes the digital divide?

The digital divide is the difference in ability to access modern technology, between those with full access and those with restricted or no access.

Question 21 mark

Which of these is a factor by which the digital divide can be judged?

The digital divide can be judged by factors such as a person's age, income and location.

Question 31 mark

Why is it difficult to give electricity to remote places such as deserts and mountains?

It is expensive to build and install cables in places hundreds of miles from power stations.

Question 41 mark

Which is a disadvantage of satellite internet connections?

Satellite signals travel through the atmosphere into space and back, so weather can disrupt them. They also cost a lot and can be slow and unreliable.

Question 51 mark

10 000 people can afford a device and have internet access, but only 5000 devices are available. How many people still cannot access a device?

Availability is how many devices there are compared with how many people want them: 10 000 − 5000 = 5000 people cannot access one.

Question 61 mark

What is digital literacy?

Digital literacy is the skill level of a person to use digital technology to find and create information, and to communicate with others using it.

Question 71 mark

Why is it cheaper for internet providers to deliver high-speed internet in cities?

Many hundreds of people live close together in cities (for example 300 people could be connected with 100 m of cable), so it is cheaper than a long connection for few rural people.

Question 81 mark

What do screen readers do?

A screen reader converts text on the screen into sound so a person can hear what is on the screen, which supports people with vision loss.

Question 91 mark

What is the challenge with Starlink-style satellite internet?

Starlink can reach places where cables and other wireless connections cannot, but satellite technology is expensive to run, so access is costly compared with ground-based access.

Question 101 mark

What does Television White Spaces (TVWS) technology use to provide internet access?

TVWS is a fixed wireless technology using often unused and unlicensed radio frequencies that currently broadcast television, useful where ground-based infrastructure is very expensive or slow to build.

Question 111 mark

Between which of these can a digital divide exist?

A digital divide can exist between countries, between different demographic groups (particular sections of the population) and between different economic areas.

Question 121 mark

Why may some older people find it hard to learn the skills needed for new technology?

Some older people may find new technology hard to learn because it works in a very different way from the technology they grew up with.

Question 131 mark

What does the Indian government initiative BharatNet aim to do?

BharatNet focuses on developing infrastructure in rural India to provide affordable internet, through more fibre optic cabling and satellite access in approximately 5300 locations.

Question 141 mark

Why do organisations such as Facebook and Google work with internet service providers to build infrastructure?

They collaborate because it means more people have access to the internet, so more people may use the services these organisations provide.
